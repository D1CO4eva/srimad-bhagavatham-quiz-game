Srimad Bhagavatam Certification Course — Session 6: Canto 1, Chapters 2-6

Suta Pauranika begins answering the 6 questions put forth in the previous chapter (see Week 5); this session covers Sage Narada and Sage Veda Vyasa's conversation.

Chapters 2 & 3: True Dharma is only that which flowers Bhakti in our hearts — practices that do not lead to devotion are ultimately in vain.
- Mundane Practice: a cycle of Dharma → Artha → Kama → Enjoyment of senses → (back to) Dharma.
- Path shown by Srimad Bhagavatam: Artha → Dharma → Bhakti → Jnana → Ananda.
- The Supreme Reality can only be attained through Bhakti, and Satsanga awakens the desire for this devotion.
- Upon concisely answering the 6 questions, Suta Pauranika says that Srimad Bhagavatam itself is the answer to these questions.

Chapter 4: Sri Shaunaka Rishi asked Suta Pauranika 4 more questions about Srimad Bhagavatam:
1. When, where, and why was this discourse of Srimad Bhagavatam held?
2. Who inspired Sri Veda Vyasa to compose Srimad Bhagavatam?
3. How did Sri Shuka, an enlightened soul, come to learn this Purana from his father?
4. Why did King Parikshith renounce his kingdom and take a vow to fast unto death, and how did he get to meet Sri Shuka and listen to the Purana from him?

Suta Pauranika starts with the avatara of Sri Veda Vyasa: Sri Vyasa was born in the Dwapara Yuga to Satyavati and Sage Parashara. Upon seeing the plight of the people in Kali Yuga and feeling dissatisfied with all his works (other Puranas, Mahabharata, etc.), he was deeply disturbed and wondered what he could do to ease their suffering.

Chapters 5 & 6: As Sage Vyasa was feeling restless, Sage Narada arrived at his ashram. When Sage Vyasa asked Sage Narada to give him clarity on why he was feeling this way, Narada pointed out that none of the works he had authored thus far were dedicated to extolling the glories and leelas of Bhagavan. He then began telling Sage Vyasa the story of his own previous birth.

Narada Maharishi's Story:
- Sage Narada was born as the son of a maid in a village, and was blessed with the satsang of sadhus during their Chaturmasya vratha.
- His ears were filled with the glories of Bhagavan during their stay, and while leaving the village the Sadhus instructed him on the highest truth.
- Soon, Narada's mother passed away and he left the village and began to meditate on Bhagavan.
- Though he had a vision of Bhagavan for just a split-second, a divine voice told him that he was not yet ready and this vision was a blessing because of his service to the Sadhus.
- He spent the rest of his days chanting the names of the Lord, and in the next life was reborn as Sage Narada, son of Brahma.

"The purpose of any Dharma is to bring a deep desire to listen to Krishna Katha." — Srimad Bhagavatam
